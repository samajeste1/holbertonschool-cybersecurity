# BioHealth Inc. — Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

**Document Reference:** BIO-POL-AUP-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** Upon execution by authorized signatory
**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) / Human Resources Director
**Scope:** All employees, contractors, consultants, and third-party personnel with access to BioHealth Inc. information systems, networks, devices, or data.

---

## Section 1: Purpose and Scope

BioHealth Inc. grants access to its information systems, networks, computing resources, and data exclusively for the conduct of authorized business activities. This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) defines the conditions under which that access is granted, the obligations of all authorized users, and the consequences of non-compliance.

Access to BioHealth systems is a privilege, not a right. It is conditional upon full compliance with this Policy, all applicable laws and regulations — including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the French Loi Informatique et Libertés, and applicable UK and German data protection legislation — and all supplementary policies issued by BioHealth management.

All users must read, understand, and acknowledge this Policy as a condition of access. Continued use of BioHealth systems constitutes acceptance of the terms herein.

---

## Section 2: User Responsibilities and Credential Management

### 2.1 Individual Accountability

Each user is solely responsible for all activity conducted under their assigned credentials. Credentials — including usernames, passwords, authentication tokens, and biometric data — are strictly personal and non-transferable.

**The following are mandatory obligations for all users:**

- Users shall not share, disclose, transfer, or otherwise make available their credentials to any other person, including colleagues, supervisors, or IT personnel.
- Users shall not use credentials belonging to another individual, whether assigned to a current employee, a former employee, or a generic shared account, unless a shared service account has been formally authorized in writing by the CISO and is subject to a documented access log.
- Users shall immediately report any suspected or confirmed compromise of their credentials to the IT Security team at `security@biohealth.io` without delay.
- Users shall not circumvent, disable, or interfere with any authentication mechanism, access control, or security monitoring tool deployed on BioHealth systems.

### 2.2 Credential Lifecycle

- Credentials for departing employees, contractors, and third-party personnel shall be revoked within **four (4) hours** of the effective date of departure or contract termination.
- The IT Security team is responsible for executing revocation. Human Resources is responsible for notifying IT Security no later than **twenty-four (24) hours** before the departure date.
- Shared credentials that cannot be attributed to a named individual are prohibited unless formally justified and documented under an exception process approved by the CISO.

## Section 4: Prohibited Activities

All authorized users of BioHealth Inc. information systems are subject to the prohibitions enumerated in this section. These prohibitions apply regardless of the user's role, seniority, employment status, or claimed justification. Ignorance of this Policy does not constitute a defense.

### Section 4.1: Unauthorized Use of Computing Resources

Users shall not use BioHealth information systems, servers, workstations, cloud infrastructure, or network bandwidth for any activity that is not directly related to the performance of their authorized job duties.

Specifically prohibited activities include, but are not limited to:

**(a) Cryptocurrency Mining**
The use of any BioHealth computing resource — including but not limited to on-premises servers, cloud instances, workstations, and virtual machines — for the purpose of cryptocurrency mining, blockchain validation, token generation, or any computationally intensive activity unrelated to BioHealth business operations is **strictly prohibited**.

This prohibition applies regardless of:
- Whether the activity occurs during or outside working hours;
- Whether the user believes the resource would otherwise be idle;
- The scale of the activity (a single process mining on one core is prohibited equally with a coordinated mining operation);
- Whether the user derives personal financial gain from the activity.

Cryptocurrency mining constitutes unauthorized consumption of company resources, creates material risk of system degradation, increases electricity and cloud infrastructure costs, and may introduce security vulnerabilities through unauthorized software installation. It is treated as a serious disciplinary matter.

**(b) Unauthorized Software Installation**
Users shall not install, execute, or cause to be installed on any BioHealth system any software, application, script, browser extension, plugin, firmware, or executable file that has not been explicitly authorized by the IT Security team through the formal Software Approval Process (BIO-PROC-SAP-001).

This prohibition includes:
- Freeware, shareware, and open-source tools not reviewed and approved by IT Security;
- Personal productivity applications, games, media players, or utilities;
- Remote access tools, VPN clients, or proxy software not issued by BioHealth;
- Hardware drivers for unauthorized peripheral devices;
- Any software obtained from unofficial, unverified, or peer-to-peer sources.

The rationale for this prohibition is security, not administrative convenience. Unapproved software may contain malicious code, introduce unpatched vulnerabilities, create unauthorized data channels, or conflict with security monitoring tools. The inability to verify the provenance and integrity of software is, in itself, a sufficient basis for prohibition.

**(c) Unauthorized Hardware Attachment**
Users shall not connect any unauthorized hardware device to BioHealth systems, including but not limited to USB storage devices, external hard drives, personal smartphones, network switches, wireless access points, or hardware implants of any kind.

## Section 4.2: Data Handling Violations

Users shall not access, copy, transfer, transmit, or disclose BioHealth data — including patient genomic data, clinical research data, employee records, and commercially sensitive information — except as explicitly authorized by their role-based access permissions and the applicable data processing agreement.

Specifically prohibited:

- Accessing genomic or patient data outside of an authorized research protocol.
- Transmitting sensitive data to personal email accounts, personal cloud storage, or any system outside the BioHealth approved environment.
- Storing sensitive data on unencrypted local devices, removable media, or personal computing equipment.
- Circumventing Data Loss Prevention (DLP) controls or any access logging mechanism.

---

## Section 5: Consequences of Violation

Violations of this Policy are treated as serious matters of professional conduct and, where applicable, legal compliance.

### 5.1 Disciplinary Consequences

Confirmed violations of this Policy shall result in disciplinary action, up to and including:

- **Written warning** for first-time, minor, or inadvertent violations where no data was compromised and no material harm occurred;
- **Suspension without pay** pending investigation for violations involving unauthorized access, data mishandling, or significant resource misuse;
- **Immediate termination of employment or contract** for violations involving cryptocurrency mining, unauthorized software installation that introduces malware, credential sharing, unauthorized access to patient data, or any intentional circumvention of security controls.

Termination decisions are made by Human Resources in consultation with the CISO and Legal Counsel. The severity of the disciplinary response shall be proportionate to the harm caused, the intent of the user, and any history of prior violations.

### 5.2 Legal Consequences

Violations that constitute criminal offenses under applicable law — including unauthorized access to computer systems (Code pénal, Articles 323-1 to 323-7), data theft, GDPR violations resulting in regulatory sanctions, or fraud — shall be reported to the competent authorities. BioHealth reserves the right to pursue civil and criminal remedies to the fullest extent permitted by law.

Regulatory violations involving patient genomic data may result in notification obligations to the CNIL, the UK ICO, or German data protection authorities, which may impose fines of up to **4% of global annual turnover** under GDPR Article 83.

### 5.3 Recovery of Losses

BioHealth reserves the right to seek financial recovery for costs directly attributable to a policy violation, including but not limited to: forensic investigation costs, regulatory fines, remediation expenses, and reputational damage quantifiable through contract loss.
